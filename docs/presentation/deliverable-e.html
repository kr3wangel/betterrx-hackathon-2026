E Deliverable E · Example scenarios

Three real situations this changes — each one runs in the demo.

Discharge readiness, a post-death pickup, and a service failure caught before the family ever notices. The measure is the phone call a human never had to make.

1

Margaret's bed is there before she is

Discharge readiness

Margaret Osei, 71, comes home to die at home. The hospital bed has to be in the living room the night before — and the hospice owns none of the bed, the truck, or the driver. They'll get the daughter's phone call either way.

Risk fires

The board flags the order before the deadline, naming the vendor's weak weekday in a sentence.

Swap

One click re-sends to a stronger vendor and clears the flag.

Confirm

The new vendor taps the magic link — accept, no login, no model.

Proof

Driver captures photo + signature — a ✓ Verified delivery, not a claim.

🛏️

The discharge stops being hostage to vendor opacity — caught while there was still time to act.

Replaces the call: "Did you get my fax? Are you actually coming?"
2

One death, one text, one trip

Post-death pickup

Ruth passes at home with two machines in the house. Today someone has to remember to call the vendor — or a hospital bed sits in a grieving family's home for days. Their own discovery has a death that never reached the vendor in time.

Nurse taps

"Passed away" at the bedside — the primary trigger, ahead of any EMR feed.

Auto-schedule

Both delivered orders flip to pickup on their own — no one dials anyone.

One text

The vendor gets one pickup text for both items — reply one digit. We batch the asking, never the answering.

One notice

The household gets one notice, not one per item.

🕊️

Per-order clocks, escalations and proofs stay intact — there is no "trip" object hiding the detail.

Replaces the call: the widow phoning about the bed still in the living room.
3

The vendor who never taps

Service-failure prevention · cold start

A brand-new vendor, entered from the hospice's own rolodex — no account, no onboarding. Two outcomes, both handled: the one who replies in prose, and the one who goes silent.

5a · taps

Types a plain-English reply in the phone sim; Claude parses it and the board flips live.

5a · low confidence

Ambiguous prose lands in the review queue instead of moving the order.

5b · silence

No tap near the deadline → an automated nag, once, never repeated.

5b · escalation

Still nothing → a case-worker escalation naming the hours. Silence became data.

🔔

The failure everyone else learns about at the front door, caught before the family notices.

Never cut from the demo: the silence variant (5b) is the differentiator.
